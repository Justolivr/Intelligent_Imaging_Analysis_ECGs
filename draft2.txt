\section{Research Goals}
\label{sec:goals}

The project addresses one main question:

\begin{quote}
\emph{To what extent can a CNN trained on rendered ECG images detect cardiac abnormalities
and rhythm disorders in real ECG images, and how can its confidence and explanations be
made reliable and understandable to users?}
\end{quote}
[TODO: replace with your own wording.] It has three parts, each tied to the gaps identified
in Chapter~\ref{ch:lit}:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{SQ1 (O1--O4):} how much does performance fall from rendered to real images,
        and how far do preprocessing and timing features reduce the fall?
  \item \textbf{SQ2 (O5):} how reliable is the model's confidence per class, and how much
        does temperature scaling improve it?
  \item \textbf{SQ3 (O6):} do the Grad-CAM explanations highlight the expected regions, and
        can students understand the confidence and explanation shown?
\end{itemize}
The preliminary hypotheses are that: (H1) performance will fall on real images, because
rendered images lack real artefacts and may allow shortcuts
\cite{geirhos_shortcut_2020}; (H2) timing features will help the rhythm classes, which depend
on beat spacing, more than the diagnostic superclasses; and (H3) temperature scaling will
lower calibration error without changing the ranking of cases \cite{guo_calibration_2017}.
Answering these questions advances understanding of whether models trained on synthetic
images can be trusted on real ones, which is the gap left by earlier image-based work
\cite{gliner_domain_2023,wang_pipeline_2025}.

\section{Requirements}
\label{sec:requirements}

The main users are university students learning ECG interpretation; a clinician view is a
secondary, optional target. Requirements come from the gaps in Chapter~\ref{ch:lit} and
from studies of how clinicians respond to explained AI output
\cite{hughes-noehrer_contrasting_2024,cabitza_painting_2023}, and are prioritised with
MoSCoW analysis. The must-have functional requirements are: upload of an ECG image;
preprocessing; flags for the nine classes (five diagnostic superclasses and four rhythm
classes) with a calibrated confidence for each; a Grad-CAM heatmap over the original image;
and a notice that the output supports review and is not a diagnosis. The must-have
non-functional requirements are per-class accuracy reporting, calibration reporting, and a
measured rendered-to-real performance gap. The full table, including should-have, could-have
and won't-have items, is in Appendix~\ref{app:moscow}.

\section{Evaluation Strategy}
\label{sec:evaluation}

\textbf{Test scenarios.} Models are tested on (i) the held-out rendered fold, using the
folds suggested with PTB-XL (folds 1--8 for training, 9 for validation and 10 for testing,
split by patient) \cite{wagner_ptb-xl_2020}, and (ii) real images from the
ECG-Image-Database \cite{reyna_ecg-image-database_2024}. The difference between (i) and (ii)
answers SQ1, and results on real images are also split by image type, such as scan and
photograph, where the numbers allow. [TODO: state which classes the real images can evaluate.]

\textbf{Metrics.} Each class is reported separately, using sensitivity, specificity,
Matthews correlation coefficient and AUROC, because the classes are imbalanced
\cite{chicco_advantages_2020}. Calibration is reported as expected calibration error per
class, with reliability diagrams before and after scaling (SQ2). Detection of the trace and
beats is checked against Pan--Tompkins applied to the source signal
\cite{pan_real-time_1985}, using the error in heart rate and RR interval.

\textbf{Comparisons.} The own CNN is compared with the pretrained model on identical splits,
and a variant without timing features tests H2. Because the rhythm classes are small
(atrial flutter has only 73 records), results are reported with bootstrap confidence
intervals and the class counts.

\textbf{Explanation and user study (SQ3).} Heatmaps are checked on labelled cases against the
expected region, and for shortcuts such as attention on the grid or printed text. The
application is tested with university students under the approved ethics application
[TODO: reference number and scope], using [TODO: tasks and a short questionnaire] to measure
whether the confidence and heatmap are understood. Clinician testing is future work.

\textbf{Success criteria.} The criteria are: calibration error is lower after scaling for
every class reported; the rendered-to-real gap is measured and reported per class; and the
detection error is within a tolerance justified by the pilot results
[TODO: set numeric targets after the pilot, relative to the baseline or to figures in the
literature, and justify each].

\textbf{Limitations.} Labels come from at most two annotators \cite{wagner_ptb-xl_2020} and
agreement between cardiologists can be poor \cite{dhutia_interrater_2017}, so scores partly
measure agreement with imperfect labels. PTB-XL reflects one period and one manufacturer.
The system supports review and does not give a diagnosis; the wider issues are discussed in
Appendix~\ref{app:ples}.

This chapter set out the research goals, requirements, tasks and evaluation strategy. The
next chapter presents the preliminary work that shows the plan is feasible.
