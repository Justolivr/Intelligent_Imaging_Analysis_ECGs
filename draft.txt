\section{Image Processing and Timing Features}

\textbf{Preprocessing (O2).} Images are prepared with the methods reviewed in
Section~\ref{sec:preprocessing}, implemented in OpenCV \cite{bradski_opencv_2000}:
contrast correction (CLAHE), noise reduction, rotation correction, and grid handling.
The grid is separated from the trace without discarding the scale needed for timing. The
choice and order of steps will be judged by their effect on classification performance
(Section~\ref{sec:medical-imaging}).

\textbf{Waveform detection and timing features.} The trace is isolated by binarisation,
grid removal and line detection, following the pipelines reviewed in
Section~\ref{sec:waveform} \cite{wu_automated_2022,wadehn_ecg_2024}. Each column of the
isolated trace is reduced to one vertical position, the R peaks are located, and the
RR intervals are converted to seconds with the grid scale. From these, heart rate and RR
regularity are calculated. Real images do not come with these values, so they are
always extracted from the image, for training and test images alike, to keep the
classifier's input consistent.

\textbf{Checking the detector.} For images whose source signal is available, the true
RR intervals are found by applying Pan--Tompkins \cite{pan_real-time_1985} to the
signal. The error between these and the image-based values (for example, mean absolute
error in heart rate) measures the accuracy of the detection step on rendered and on real
images. [TODO: add the accuracy target stated in O2.]

\section{Calibration and Explanation}
\label{sec:method-calib}

\textbf{Calibration (O5).} After training, each class score is divided by a temperature
fitted on the validation fold (Equation~\ref{eq:temperature-multilabel}), which changes
the probabilities but not the ranking of cases \cite{guo_calibration_2017}. Calibration
is reported per class, before and after scaling.

\textbf{Explanation (O6).} Grad-CAM heatmaps \cite{selvaraju_gradcam_2017} are produced
for each flagged class. They are checked on labelled cases against the region where the
abnormality is expected, for example the QRS complex for a conduction disturbance, and
for shortcuts such as attention on the grid or printed text
\cite{geirhos_shortcut_2020}. This check is qualitative.

\section{Evaluation}

\textbf{Classification (O3, O4).} Each class is evaluated separately, using sensitivity,
specificity, Matthews correlation coefficient and AUROC, since the classes are imbalanced
\cite{chicco_advantages_2020}. The main comparison is between the held-out rendered test
fold and the real images, and the difference between them measures the generalisation gap
(Section~\ref{sec:medical-imaging}). Both models are evaluated in the same way.

\textbf{Calibration (O5).} Expected calibration error is reported per class, with
reliability diagrams before and after temperature scaling.

\section{Tools and Risks}

The system is implemented in Python, with OpenCV for image processing and
[TODO: PyTorch] for the networks and for Grad-CAM, and a [TODO: GUI framework] interface. The main risks are:
atrial flutter has few examples, so its results may be unstable; labels come from at
most two annotators \cite{wagner_ptb-xl_2020}; models trained on rendered images may not
transfer to real ones; and the preprocessing may fail on poor images. They are addressed
by reporting per-class results with the class counts, by measuring the rendered-to-real
gap explicitly, and by evaluating the detection step separately on real images.
